The training run, as a train

Second direction, replacing the conveyor concepts. The problem with the belt was time: stages take hours, so constant motion reads as fake. On a railway line, dwelling at a station is the natural state. The run ("r7") sits at a stop with a live dwell timer and stage telemetry; departures are rare, real events. A held run is a train held at REVIEW on a siding, exactly what happened to r5.

All four styles share the same mechanics: seven stations, one train per run, a telemetry card that carries the liveliness (loss curve, step counter, elapsed time), signals per station, and a departure board that lists every run honestly, including the held and experimental ones. When no run is active, the line simply shows the last released train at the terminus.

demo note: dwell that takes hours in reality lasts seconds here; the timers display the real magnitudes. later the GPU pod posts {run, stage, detail} and the page just renders it.

Why this fits the time profile

Dwelling is the default state of a railway, so a visitor who arrives mid-run sees a stopped train with a growing dwell timer and live telemetry, and that reads as truthful rather than stuck. Departures are rare and therefore feel like events, which is exactly what a stage transition is. Holds have a native vocabulary (a siding, a held service), multiple runs are just multiple trains, and an idle line with the last released train at the terminus is a perfectly normal thing to look at.

The integration stays the same as before: the pod posts {run, stage, detail} per transition, the page polls it, and history (scores, suites, the run log with its failures) renders from model/site/public-data.json.
